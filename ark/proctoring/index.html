Ark · Proctoring

The proctor is code on your computer. Not a stranger in a cloud.

Traditional remote proctoring sends your face, your screen, and your room to a foreign server, where an unknown algorithm — or an unknown human — decides whether you cheated. The Attestto Ark Station inverts the model: every byte of your exam stays on your machine, and the algorithm that judges you is open source.

How it works ← Station modules
The problem

Cloud proctoring shouldn't be the only option

Examity, ProctorU, Honorlock, Respondus — every major remote proctoring vendor follows the same pattern: install a browser extension, hand over your camera and microphone, and let a stranger or a third-party AI watch your exam in real time on a server you don't control. The video is processed in foreign jurisdictions, retained for years, and occasionally leaked. For high-stakes exams in countries with strong data-sovereignty rules, that model is structurally incompatible with the law and with basic dignity.

The Ark proctoring module is the alternative: a white-box, local-only proctor that gives the institution forensic-grade exam integrity without ever uploading the candidate's video, screen, or biometric data. The institution gets a signed, hashed, tamper-evident exam record. The candidate keeps their privacy. The auditor gets source code.

How it works

Three layers of local integrity

The proctoring module runs as a sub-system of the Attestto Ark Station — the same Electron desktop that holds your vault and signs your credentials. Because the Station has Node.js underneath the Chromium engine, it can do things a normal browser cannot: directly read camera frames, monitor the desktop, inspect running processes, and write a tamper-evident log to disk.

🎥

1. Direct hardware access

The Station uses standard Web APIs to open the camera, but processes the video locally on the device's CPU/GPU to detect faces and verify liveness. The original frames never leave the machine.

  • Camera + face detection on-device
  • Liveness via blink + head-pose
  • Screen monitoring via Electron desktopCapturer
  • Process inspection via Node.js
🛡

2. White box, not black box

Traditional proctoring (Examity, ProctorU) sends your video to foreign servers for AI analysis. In the Ark, the original video never leaves your device — the "AI" runs inside the Electron shell on your machine, and the algorithm is open source.

  • No cloud video upload, ever
  • No foreign-jurisdiction storage
  • Open-source detection models
  • Auditable by any university or regulator
🔒

3. Forensic integrity layer

Once the exam ends, the Station creates a SHA-256 hash of the entire session — anomaly logs, timestamps, screen-state digests, biometric confidence scores — and signs it with the candidate's Ed25519 vault key. The institution receives a signed, byte-frozen receipt.

  • SHA-256 session hash
  • Ed25519 signature from vault
  • Tamper-evident anomaly log
  • Verifiable on verify.attestto.com
White box vs black box

Two execution models

The decisive difference is not what the proctor watches, it is where the watching happens and who can audit the watcher.

⛔  Cloud proctoring

  • Video uploaded to vendor's server
  • Stored in foreign jurisdiction
  • Reviewed by unknown humans or proprietary AI
  • No source-code access for institutions
  • Institution has no forensic guarantee — only the vendor's word

✅  Ark white-box proctoring

  • Video processed on the candidate's device
  • Original frames never leave the machine
  • Detection models are open source and auditable
  • Institution receives a signed, hashed exam receipt
  • Independent auditor can verify the entire pipeline

Technical summary

The proctoring module is part of the Attestto Ark Station — the same Electron desktop that holds your vault and signs your documents. There is no separate "proctoring app" to install. Every module shares one vault, one identity, and one auditable codebase.

Engine

Electron + Vue 3. Chromium for the UI, Node.js for hardware. Cross-platform desktop binary.

Detection

Local face + screen monitoring. Camera frames and screen captures stay on the device. Zero cloud video.

Vault security

xsalsa20-poly1305 + scrypt. Encrypted local key store. Vault key is the proctoring receipt signer.

Receipt signing

Ed25519 over SHA-256 session hash. Tamper-evident exam record verifiable on any Attestto verifier.

Governance

3-of-4 multisig (planned). Trust framework keys are split across institutional shareholders. No single party can rewrite records.

License

Open source. Apache 2.0. Read the source. Build it yourself. Verify the binary matches.

FAQ

Hard questions, honest answers

Can the Station spy on me beyond the exam?

No. The proctoring module only activates inside an exam session that the candidate explicitly starts, and stops the moment the session ends. Because the source is open, any auditor can verify that the camera and screen capture pipelines are not running outside an active exam.

What if my computer crashes mid-exam?

The session log is written incrementally to disk and signed at every checkpoint. A crash produces a partial signed record up to the last checkpoint. The institution decides whether to allow a re-take based on the partial record — there is no proctoring vendor to blame.

Where is the exam video stored?

Nowhere outside your machine. The Ark never uploads raw video. What the institution receives is a cryptographically signed log of events (face detected, screen anomaly, process opened), never the underlying frames.

Who issues my Ed25519 signing key?

You do. The Station generates the keypair locally on first launch, stores it in the encrypted vault, and never transmits it to Attestto or anyone else. The corresponding public key is what verifiers use to confirm a session record came from your machine.

How does the institution trust the receipt?

Each receipt embeds the candidate's public key, the SHA-256 of the session log, the timestamps, and the Ed25519 signature. The institution verifies the signature against the candidate's enrolled public key — the same way verify.attestto.com verifies signed PDFs.

Can I read the proctoring code?

Yes — every line. The Station, the verifier, and the wallet adapter are public on GitHub under Apache 2.0. If anything looks wrong, file an issue or open a coordinated disclosure to security@attestto.com.
